## What it does

Borrowers and loan officers on a mortgage-lending platform upload closing
packets, pay stubs, bank statements and dozens of other document types.
Getting structured, field-level data out of them reliably — not "usually" —
is the difference between straight-through processing and a human re-keying
everything by hand.

## What I built

The incumbent OCR vendor was doing real work: it extracted roughly 66% of
the documents in the pipeline at 80–90% accuracy. The remaining third it
only classified — those documents were never extracted at all. The project
was to prove the vendor could be replaced, by testing competing theories
about how to split, index, categorize and extract documents more reliably,
with more coverage, and cheaper.

Across those experiments, field accuracy measured against a human-graded
ground truth went from roughly 30% on the first pass to 87% — at least as
reliable as the vendor's scores, extending coverage past the third of
documents it never extracted, with better categorization and indexing, at
about 3% of the vendor's cost.

The pipeline is not a single prompt. It is a set of typed extraction
contracts per document type, a routing layer that decides which lane a
document takes, and a scoring step that measures every lane against the
same human-graded ground truth instead of trusting any of them.

I led the team evaluating vision-LLM document classification and extraction
for the replacement, personally designing and testing the competing
pipeline architectures — which made 80+ page closing packets processable
at a small fraction of the vendor's per-loan cost.

## Why it mattered

Every extraction claim here is backed by a measured before/after on real
documents, not a demo. That distinction — an AI feature that is tested,
scored and cost-traced like any other production system — is the throughline
of most of what I have shipped with LLMs since.
